BLAU Eatery & Coffee · Kelapa Gading, Jakarta

Five ways the site could look. Pick one.

Each direction below is a complete world — ground, typeface, photography treatment and pacing all moving together. Mixing two is how sites end up looking like everyone else's, so this board exists to narrow it to one.

Every mock uses BLAU's real photography from the 2025 menu and the brand's own indigo, #414889. Same brief throughout: one walk-in eatery, 75 menu items, a real coffee program, WhatsApp as the primary call to action.

A

Editorial Quiet

Partial fit

Whitespace is the design. Type stays small and refined. Photography is full-bleed and rare. The restraint itself does the signalling.

References

  • Locavore NXTInspected · your pick Monochrome logo, nature-led full-bleed imagery, and progressive disclosure — sections expand rather than dumping everything at once.
  • AtomixInspected Centred logo, four nav links, black on white, near-zero decoration. Proof that a two-Michelin restaurant fits on essentially one page.
  • Sunday in BrooklynInspected Closest to BLAU's scale. Photography carries everything; hours split by meal period; booking embedded inline rather than linked out.
  • NomaUnverified The reference everyone cites for whitespace-as-luxury. Blocked my fetch, so open it yourself.

BLAU in this direction

Near-white ground. The indigo appears once on the entire page. Wordmark small and centred, menu set at 15px with heavy leading, hours as plain text rather than in a card.

The risk

BLAU is a 38k-coffee neighbourhood eatery. Taken neat this reads as expensive and unwelcoming — and the kids-meal crowd bounces.

B

Warm Narrative

Strong fit · highest effort

Texture, story and appetite. Busy on purpose. Culture is the differentiator — not minimalism.

References

  • DishoomInspected The strongest single reference on this board. An all-day eatery, Awwwards-winning. Menus split by meal period as separate routed pages, a persistent booking CTA, and a founding myth per location set in a different moment of Indian history.
  • OttolenghiInspected Serif wordmark against sans body, modular card grid, horizontal-scroll collections. Colour enters only through photography — terracottas, greens, golds — never through graphic elements.
  • Kopi KalyanUnverified Closest business-model match in Jakarta: coffee and a real food menu, vintage-eclectic warmth, own roasting.

BLAU in this direction

Warm off-white ground with indigo as the anchor rather than the star. Photography wall to wall. Menu split into Rice Bowls, Pasta & Pizza, Small Plates, Coffee and Cold Drinks — each with its own URL.

The risk

It needs volume. A lot of photography, including interiors, and a lot of written copy. BLAU has food shots only and no story text yet, so this is the most work by a distance.

C

Cool Precision

Partial fit

Swiss grid, cold neutrals, type doing all the work. The coffee-professional aesthetic.

References

  • %ArabicaInspected Geometric deltas in the wordmark, high-contrast greyscale, five nav links, video-forward. Premium achieved by subtraction.
  • Common GroundsInspected Jakarta. Neutral premium palette, and crucially Roastery and Archive as their own pages — it proves the coffee program is real by giving it a room.
  • Onyx Coffee LabInspected Off-whites, deep black text, dramatically letter-spaced caps, alternating left/right hero sections. Every coffee gets flavour notes, producer and origin geography.
  • 1/15 CoffeeUnverified Scandinavian-inspired Jakarta chain — the closest visual cousin to BLAU's cool palette.

BLAU in this direction

Strict grid, letter-spaced caps, indigo used as a system colour — rules, active states, the wordmark. The V60 guest-bean program gets its own page, treated as seriously as Onyx treats theirs.

The risk

Cold. BLAU's menu is genuinely funny — Kopi Susu Bro, Black Magic, Bagoge. A Swiss grid flattens that, and the food half of the business ends up feeling secondary to the coffee.

D

Cinematic Dark

Blocked on assets

Dark ground, video first, atmosphere over information. The most immediately impressive option on the board.

References

  • ISMAYAInspected · your pick Dark grounds, monochrome logo, video backgrounds inside section headers, and brands sorted into mood pillars — Elevate, Unite, Ignite, Everyday, Wellbeing — rather than by cuisine.
  • %ArabicaInspected Also lives here: a dedicated Films section sits in the primary nav, with a video player anchoring the homepage.

BLAU in this direction

The ink from the actual menu cover, #2D283D, becomes the site ground rather than just the cover. Wordmark reversed in white exactly as the print does it. Hero is footage of the bar — V60 pouring, sea salt foam going onto a latte.

Why it's blocked

Two hard dependencies that don't exist yet. BLAU's entire photo library is shot on white plaster and does not read on a dark ground — the mock above only works because the oxtail happens to sit on dark wood. And there is no video at all.

E

Graphic Block

Strong fit · lowest effort

Oversized type, flat colour fields, hard edges. The fast-casual challenger look — and the one BLAU is already designed in.

References

  • BLAU's own 2025 menuInspected This is the honest one. Big indigo headings, flat white fields, no rules, no ornament, photography floating in white space. BLAU already works in this style — it just hasn't been carried to the web.
  • Kopi KenanganInspected Indonesian conventions at scale: themed drink "series" as a merchandising slot, an outlet finder on Maps, WhatsApp as consumer service.
  • Sweetgreen · Nando's · FeastablesUnverified The fast-casual camp named in 2026 trend coverage — custom type and colour systems used specifically to escape category defaults.

BLAU in this direction

Indigo at display scale, headings large enough to crop. Flat indigo panels alternating with white, menu categories as full-width colour blocks, prices set large and tabular, and the cut-through A from the wordmark pulled out as a graphic device.

The risk

It tips into loud and cheap the moment the photography isn't strong enough to hold it. BLAU's photography is strong enough.

E as the frame, B as the voice.

Direction E isn't a style I'd be imposing on BLAU — it's the style BLAU's own menu is already designed in. Big indigo headings, flat white, generous air, photography floating free. Carrying that to the web means the site and the printed menu read as visibly the same brand, and it's the only direction fully buildable with the assets that exist today.

Then borrow B's substance where E runs thin: Dishoom's menu architecture — separate routed pages cut by how people actually eat, with a persistent CTA — and Dishoom's willingness to tell one specific cultural story instead of a generic one.

What that combination avoids: every other Jakarta cafe defaults to warm brown-and-cream. Nobody in this category is using indigo at display scale. It is the cheapest way available to look like nobody else.

The one question that decides it

Do you want BLAU to read quiet and considered (A or C), or warm and appetising (B or E)? They produce genuinely different sites, and I'd rather you picked than have me guess.
